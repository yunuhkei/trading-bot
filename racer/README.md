# Racer prototype

A free-roam arcade racer in **one self-contained HTML file** (`Racer_Prototype.html`, no libraries, no network).
It is built from the modules in `racer/src` by `python3 racer/build.py` (the output is committed so you can just open it).

* **Map**: one fixed county (a 10.2 km square heightmap with 5 m cells, 8.5 km of it land: the sea is to the west and south, cliffs and a beach to the east and north), roads authored in plan view only (`src/20_map.js`), heights follow the land and the land is carved around the roads:
  a ~10 km highway ring with sweeping bends (and a long straight), a mountain pass with switchbacks, a twisty coast road (Highway 1 style) with an overpass,
  a long straight on the southern plains, an east link, and the county's districts, inspired by San Francisco and California:
  **Downtown** = two street grids (one axis-aligned, one rotated to follow the diagonal **Market Street**, like SF), a tower district with a pyramid tower,
  Victorian row houses on **Nob Hill / Russian Hill** (grades over 20 percent) with a hairpin **Lombard Street** block, **Twin Peaks** with a winding road,
  **Golden Gate Park** (a long green strip with a lake and a park drive); **the Valley** = LA style wide palm-lined boulevards with strip malls, parking lots and stucco houses;
  **Mulholland** = a very twisty ridge road in the hills; **Santa Cruz** = a beach town on the south coast (bungalows, motels, a boardwalk with a Ferris wheel,
  lifeguard towers, umbrellas and a pier you can drive out on); **Palm Springs** = a desert resort town of wide palm boulevards (mid-century hotels round pools,
  a golf course, villas with pools).
  **The wider county** (`src/20_world.js`, terrain in `baseH`): a northern range with snow above about 360 m, a 75 m mesa in the north-east cut by a winding canyon (the canyon road follows the floor to a turnaround on the rim),
  a desert lake, a farm lake, and five small towns plus two industrial areas that fill the gaps (**Pacifica** on the coast road, **Fresno** farm town, **Barstow** desert town, **Big Pines** mountain village on a terrace in the foothills,
  the **harbour** industrial area, **Mojave Works**), each a street grid ringed by a boulevard like the districts (`makeTown`), with farms (barn, silo, house, shed, field) along the farm roads, more suburbs round the Valley and the towns,
  and 17 **safe houses** (a garage with a tall green sign, green dots on the map; behaviour comes later). New highways: the east highway to Barstow and the loop back through the refinery and the southern highway, the north highway over the mountain-pass loop to Big Pines, the farm roads.
  **Network** (one connected road system, checked by a script that reports roads lying on top of each other, dead ends and disconnected pieces): every district is ringed
  by a boulevard (`perimeter()`), every street of the district ends on it, and the highways join the boulevards: Geary and the park drive meet downtown's boulevard,
  the boulevard road runs from downtown across the hills to the ring, the Valley joins it and the ring, the coast road runs from Market Street's south-west end
  round the coast to the southern highway (past the Santa Cruz link), the east link joins the ring to the southern highway, Palm Springs joins the east link and the southern
  highway, Mulholland runs from the ring to the boulevard road and the mountain pass leaves the ring and returns to it. Branches leave a road along its side and peel away
  (`rampPts`) or end as a T on its edge (`edgePt`), so no road is built on top of another; where roads really cross (coast road over the ring) there is an overpass
  (found and built automatically, `fixCrossings`).
  Collision: there are no guardrails for now (`RAILS=false` in `src/21_terrain.js` brings back the highway, cliff and bridge rails); buildings (also the rotated ones), poles, trees and bridge piers are solid
  (with height ranges, so the deck of a bridge is not blocked by what is under it); steep banks are walls.
  Hills: the road-surface lookup picks the nearest road segment at the car's own level (never by height alone, which made the ground a staircase), every city street takes
  its height from one shared smoothed height field (so crossing streets are flush), connector roads blend to the street they join, the body height follows the ground
  closely and the ground cannot launch the car faster than the slope allows. The tests that measure this are in `racer/tests/` (see its README for how to run them and the baseline numbers the driving feel must keep). Everything is deterministic.
* **Roads are smooth**: the height profile of every road (not the city streets, which follow a smoothed height field) is smoothed with a 28 m binomial filter after the grade limit (`vSmooth` in `src/20_map.js`, ends held exactly so roads still meet flush): the slope clamps used to leave kinks (a slope flipping between +22 % and +6 %, crests only 16 m in radius that threw the car into the air on the mountain pass). No crest tighter than 35 m remains except the designed jump hills (39 m).
* **Lanes**: lane width is one constant (`LANE_W`, 3.6 m in `src/00_util.js`); `laneInfo(hw,style)` gives lanes per direction from the road width, and both the road shader (painted dashed lines, centre line / median) and the traffic lane graph use it, so the paint and the AI lanes always agree. Markings fade towards junctions.
* **Lane graph** (`src/23_lanes.js`, built once at boot in about 0.1 s, 482 lane edges, 2,377 turn links, 434 lane-km, 2 MB): one centre-line per lane per direction for every road except the patches (Lombard Street, the pier), stored in travel order. Junctions are found where a road end touches another road (T, ramp, end to end, three roads at one point) and where two roads cross at the same level; a turn is a short cubic curve from one lane to another (right turns from the outer lane, left turns from the inner lane, a lane that runs out of road may turn from any lane; no left turns across a highway median, so highways get a U-turn crossover about every 500 m; dead ends get a U-turn; a curve that would be shorter than 8 m is pushed further out). Each lane point has a comfortable speed from the road type, curvature and grade. `node racer/tests/lanecheck.js` checks it (the road directions form one strongly connected piece, so a car can never get trapped).
* **Traffic** (`src/60_traffic.js` simulation, `src/61_trafficgl.js` cars): up to 56 cars (`?tmax=N` changes it, `?traffic=0` turns it off) spawned out of sight 140-430 m from the player and removed beyond 540 m (or behind the player past 300 m); more cars in town (downtown 56, the Valley 51, towns 37, highways about 22-27, mountain roads about 19-22). Each car has a horizon (12 points, 6-10 m apart, 66-110 m ahead) along its lane and the turn it picked at the next junction (a seeded hash, so it never changes its mind); IDM car following with a speed profile from the lane speeds ahead; it gives way by comparing its rectangle with every other car's rectangle at the same moment over the next 4 s (road class and turn type decide who goes, then who is nearer; a car that has given way keeps doing so for a second; anything about to touch while still moving brakes whatever the priority); it changes lane to get into the lane of its turn, and to get past a slow car; swerves and brakes for the player; steers by pure pursuit (a kinematic bicycle). A car that has been waiting for a few seconds goes anyway, and a jam that lasts 12-22 s away from the player is dissolved. Five body types (sedan, hatch, SUV, van, police car) in 12 colours with head and tail lights (brake lights light up), drawn instanced with two levels of detail: cars within 52 m of the camera (34 m on Low) use a **smooth body made by the same generator as the player's cars** (about 2.8k triangles: rounded flanks, shoulders and roof, glass, wheels with rims, no facets), the rest the old 200-triangle body; one draw per type and level (up to 10 draws). Everything is deterministic (one seeded generator, fixed 60 Hz tick shared with the 120 Hz player step).
* **Crashes**: a hit is a real collision between two rectangles (mass 1.1-2.3 t, restitution .26, friction .32) with an impulse at the contact point, so a hit car spins. A light bump just slows the car; above 2.5 kJ it becomes a wreck (a free body: slides, spins, stops at walls and trees, stays up to 30 s and goes away sooner once out of sight). Every impact is recorded in `IMP` (`40_car.js`): source (0 building/tree/pole, 1 steep bank, 2 landing, 3 traffic), closing speed, **energy lost in joules**, contact point and normal, the car's zone (0 front, 1 rear, 2 left, 3 right), the other mass. The damage system reads `IMP`.
* **Damage** (`src/62_damage.js`): every impact in `IMP` adds its energy to one of five zones (front, rear, left, right, roof; a zone is destroyed by `DZ_CAP` = 650 / 520 / 420 / 420 / 320 kJ, a sedan at 24 m/s into a parked car is about 180 kJ; 14 % of a hit spills into the neighbouring zones; scrapes below 3.5 m/s or 8 kJ and landings below 11 m/s do nothing). The zones feed **one health bar** (HEALTH, above the NITRO bar) and decide where the dents and the engine smoke go. **Damage never changes how the car drives** (`DMG.spd/acc` stay 1, `pull/leak` 0): it is health and looks only; the car is wrecked when the front or rear is gone or health is under 16 %. **Full dents**: the real vertices of the car body near the contact are pushed in along the direction of the hit (Gaussian of the pristine position, up to 0.34 m deep, never more than 0.5 m from where they were, noise a function of position so no cracks open), normals are shaken so the dent catches the light and the paint scuffs; the body buffer is re-uploaded with `bufferSubData` (at once for a big hit, at most every 0.2 s for a run of small ones). Repair puts the pristine copy back. **Traffic cars are dented too**: each car keeps two dents (centre on its body, depth, radius) from its crashes (`trDent` in `60_traffic.js`); the traffic vertex shader pushes the metal in towards the middle of the car, darkens and dulls it there.
* **Safe houses** (`src/63_safe.js`): the garages on the map each have a glowing pad in front of the roller door (green = yours, red = locked). Park on it (under 9 m/s, 1.5 s, the pad slows you) and the car is repaired, the heat is cleared and the spot is saved. The Home Garage near the start is free and owned from the first second; the others are locked. **The money system is NOT decided**: `ECON` is a set of hooks that do nothing (`price(id)` returns null, `canBuy/buy` return false, `repairCost` is 0, `onBusted/onWrecked/onRepair/onUnlock` are empty) and `SAFE.price[id]` is null for every house but Home. Call `safeUnlock(id)` once a house has been paid for; progress is kept in `localStorage` (`racer.save`). Wrecked or busted: a 3.4 s end screen, then you respawn on the pad of the nearest garage you own, repaired and with no cops. `?safe=all` unlocks every garage for testing.
* **Cops** (`src/64_cops.js`, cars from `60_traffic.js` with body type 4): heat 1-5 (stars). **You are chased for three things only**: you hit a cop car, you blow past a cop at 120 mph (53.6 m/s) or more (within 55 m of it), or you crash into another car (`copTrigger(level)`; nothing else, not even being seen speeding, adds heat). A crash into a car is worth 2 stars (3 for a big one or if it is wrecked), hitting a cop 3, wrecking a cop 4; more crimes while wanted add to it. Difficulty presets in `COPD` (`?cops=off|medium|hard`; **Forgiving is the default**). **They do not give up**: a chaser matches your speed times 1.1 (up to 105 m/s, never less than the preset's top) with a rubber-band boost when it falls behind, accelerates at 12 m/s^2 (it used to be 3.4, so it could never catch you), always knows where you are (no line of sight needed to keep chasing), takes the real route to you (A* over the lane graph, recomputed every 1.8 s, so it takes the right turns and u-turns instead of greedy guesses), and goes into free pursuit (over the grass, round you) when it is within 115 m with line of sight or 40 m. A chaser that falls more than 520 m behind is replaced by a fresh one 260-350 m behind you; the first chasers spawn 120-220 m behind on the road you are on. **You only lose them by being more than 230 m (Forgiving) from every chaser and out of sight for 14 s**, by reaching a garage, or by wrecking them; **they attack from the first stars**: rams from heat 2 on Forgiving and heat 1 on Medium and Hard (any chaser within 85 m aims at your rear quarter faster than you: 10 m/s faster and 4.2 s between rams on Forgiving, 12 and 3 s on Medium, 15 and 2.2 s on Hard; a bot that never swerves is wrecked or busted by them in about 80 s on Forgiving), roadblocks from heat 3 (Forgiving, Medium) or 2 (Hard), more chasers (Forgiving 0,2,3,4,4,5 by heat, Medium 0,2,4,5,6,7, Hard 1,3,5,6,7,8); boxing you in at under 6 m/s for 5 s busts you. Patrols (3 / 4 / 5, 70 % spawning ahead of you) flash their light bar slowly. Sirens, bold red/blue glows, a coloured wash on the road, edge markers showing where chasers are, stars and bust meter on the HUD, flashing dots on the minimap. The police car has a light bar with red, clear and blue segments, a push bar, A-pillar spotlights, an aerial and a livery: white doors, roof and pillars, black bonnet, wings and boot, a blue and gold stripe and a badge on each door.
* **Navigation and the map** (`src/65_nav.js`): the **world map is touchable**: drag to move it, pinch (or mouse wheel) to zoom, tap a road to drop a pin, `SET WAYPOINT` to go there (`CLEAR WAYPOINT`, `MY CAR`, `CLOSE MAP`). The route is found with A* over the lane graph (it follows the way traffic really drives: one-way lanes, turns that exist, median crossovers; 3-11 ms for any route on the map), starts in the direction you are heading, and is **drawn on the road** as a pulsing blue ribbon for the next 420 m with a pillar of light at the waypoint; the HUD shows `WAYPOINT 4.4 km` under the minimap and an arrow along the top edge when it is not in front of you; the minimap and the world map show the route and the flag. Leave the route (a wrong turn) and a new route is made from where you are; arrive (26 m) and it clears.
* **NPC damage**: each traffic car keeps up to 3 dents from its crashes (position, depth, radius; the vertex shader pushes the metal in and crumples and dulls it) and a damage total (`c.dmg`, 0..1; a sedan is finished by 170 kJ, a van 280 kJ). From 0.45 its head and tail lights are smashed (dark), from 0.45 it smokes (more from 0.65, fire from 0.85); a light knock (under 32 kJ, damage under 0.72) spins it round and it re-attaches to the nearest lane and **drives on, limping** (slower and weaker the more it is damaged), a big hit finishes it as a wreck.
* **Drift taps build up** (`40_car.js`, `carStep`): tap the drift pedal again within 1.6 s of the last tap while still sliding and the slide gets harder each time (`C.tapN` 0..5, smoothed to `C.dbK` 0..1): the slide angle the tap kicks out grows up to 2.5 times, the slide window 2.2 times, the yaw-rate cap 1.55 times, grip in the slide drops by 40 %, and the drift points go up to 3 times (the toast shows `x2`, `x3` ...). The build-up fades 1.6 s after the last tap, taps made slower than that start again and a single tap or a hold feels exactly as before (`dbK` is 0). On flat ground at 94 mph: one tap 10 degrees, three taps 20, five taps 48 (`rdrift3.js`).
* **Damage you can see** (`src/66_parts.js`, `cgParts` in `70_carmesh.js`): looks only, nothing here is read by the driving. The generator finds the **bonnet, boot lid, both doors (with their windows), both bumpers (with the lights) and both mirrors** by where each triangle sits on the car (quads together, so edges follow the grid), copies them into vertices of their own and puts a dark liner a few centimetres inside the skin behind every door, bonnet and boot (so an open door shows a dark cabin). The index buffer is ordered by part, so each part is one run, drawn with its own hinge matrix (the player's car is 1 + up to 8 draws, the glass the same). Each part follows a target taken from the damage zones with thresholds that change after every repair (`partTarget`): the bonnet lifts as the front is hurt (up to 63 degrees) and flutters at speed, the boot lid the same for the rear, a door opens on the side that was hit (up to 80 degrees, held back by the wind at speed, swinging when hit), bumpers swing and sag, mirrors fold; a hit kicks the nearest parts and a latch that broke holds the part a little open. **Parts tear off** (`partDetach`): a bonnet or boot lid after a hit over 300 kJ with the zone past 60 % or at 90 %, a bumper over 55-75 % damage, a door over 85 % (or 300 kJ at 70 %), a mirror from 25 kJ; the piece keeps part of the car's speed, is thrown (bonnet up and back, doors sideways) and tumbles under gravity, bounces and slides on the real ground (`groundAt`), lies there, and goes after about 19 s (at most 10 pieces). **Glass cracks** (`uCr` in the car shader): a cell pattern of lines spreads from a point as the zone beside the window takes damage (windscreen from the front and roof, rear glass from the rear, each side from its side). **Lights die** one side at a time (`uDm`: the emissive head or tail light goes dark, the glow sprite and half the headlight pool go too). Repair (`dmgReset`) puts everything back on and starts a new random set. **Traffic and police cars** open their bonnet (front hit), boot (rear) or door (side) once the car is hurt: part numbers ride in the high bits of the material byte (`part * 16 + material`), flag bits 16/32/64/128 in the instance flags, and the vertex shader swings the part about the hinge (`uHg`, per body type) with a little flutter; the liner is part of the same mesh.
* **Scenery** (`buildScenery` in `src/22_build.js`, runs once at boot, deterministic, adds about 0.7 s): along every open road, counting from the road's own shoulder: **telegraph poles with two wires** along one side of two-lane roads, **white reflector posts** along the highways, **gantry signs** (two legs, a beam, a green panel with text bars for each carriageway) about every 1.1 km, **billboards on stilts** facing the road about every 0.7 km, **speed limit signs**, **yellow chevron boards on the outside of bends**, **fences** along stretches of green country, **tree belts** (a noise field decides where they are dense; 2-6 hero trees per cluster), **bushes**, in the dry south and east **cacti, boulders and scrub** instead, **wind turbines** in rows of three 70-190 m off the road (58), **gas stations** (canopy, pumps, a shop, a tall price sign that glows), **farmsteads** (barn, silo, house) or in the desert an adobe house with a **water tower**, and **high-voltage pylon lines** (two legs, a cross beam, three wires) beside the highways. Everything keeps off other roads (`nearRoadXZ`), off steep ground and water, and is solid where it should be (`COLC`/`COLO`). Counts on the shipped map: 363 poles, 954 reflector posts, 25 gantries, 42 billboards, 105 speed signs, 64 chevrons, 1,428 tree belts (5,129 trees), 2,392 bushes, 246 boulder groups, 382 cacti, 58 turbines, 8 gas stations, 49 pylons (`rscenery.js`). Draw distances: furniture 380-560 m, buildings and big landmarks 800-1250 m, trees 260-420 m (`R.drawProp`). **Better trees** (`ptree`): firs of three overlapping tiers in varied dark greens, oaks with a second lobe, narrow poplars, desaturated greens and the odd autumn tree, for roadside, city, park and town trees; the dense forests keep the light two-tier trees (they are 14,000 of them) so the extra detail costs about 1.8 times the tree triangles of before (826k on the whole map, only what is near is drawn).
* **Graphics pass: weather and time of day** (`ENVP`, `envApply` in `src/30_render.js`; the grade, sky and clouds are driven by numbers, nothing is hard-coded to one look): three looks, chosen with the **SKY** button on the HUD (or `?env=day|overcast|sunset`, remembered): **DAY** (the default: a high warm sun at about 37 degrees, a blue sky with scattered cumulus, clean air, a dry road, hard shadows, saturation 1.14), **OVERCAST** (the old gloomy look: pale sun behind cloud, thick grey haze, wet road, saturation .72) and **SUNSET** (the sun 9 degrees up, orange, long shadows, a glowing haze, a part-wet road, lit windows). A look sets: sun direction and colour, sky and horizon colours, fog density and sun glow, the grade (`uGr`: saturation, cool/warm split, contrast, exposure), the cloud colours and how much sky they cover (`uSk`, `uClA`, `uClB`), the sun disk and halo (`uSd`), road wetness, the share of lit windows and the strength of the sun shadows.
* **Reflections come from the real sky** (`skyCubeInit` / `skyCubeUpdate`): the sky shader (a `CUBE` variant that writes linear light, stored as sqrt(c/4), with a dark ground below the horizon) is drawn into the six faces of a 128 x 128 cube map, mip-mapped, when the weather changes and every 7 s for the drift of the clouds (about 6 small draws). The car paint, clear coat and glass, the road (wet or dry), the water, the traffic and the building glass sample it (`envl(r, roughness)` picks the blur through the mip level), so clouds and the sun show in the bonnet and the roof. A GPU that cannot render into a cube face gets a CPU gradient instead. The sun also has an analytic glint on the paint. **Cloud shadows** drift over the terrain and the road (`csh`: the same kind of cloud field as the sky, strong only in direct sun).
* **Terrain and road detail** (tiers Medium and High; the `DET` shader variant): grass in patches of yellower and deeper green, blades and clumps close up, small bumps that catch the sun (a normal from three noise taps); asphalt with aggregate grain, darker repair patches, hairline cracks, darker wheel tracks in every lane and seams across the road every 23 m.
* **Foliage** (`pcard`, `pcrown`, `pstar`, `ptuft`, `makeFoliageTex`): a leaf atlas painted on the CPU from a seeded generator (broadleaf cluster, fir needles with a ragged lower edge, bush, grass blades). **Oaks and poplars** are a trunk with boughs and a crown of 5-12 alpha-cut leaf cards whose normals point away from the middle of the crown (so a crown shades like a ball), **firs** are three star-shaped cones (points alternate long and short) with the needle texture, **palms** have arching fronds cut from the grass-blade tile, **bushes** are five cards, and about 8,000 **grass tufts** line the verges. Foliage sways in the wind (the weight is kept in the vertex colour alpha, `uT` in the prop vertex shader), is lit wrapped and glows a little with the sun behind it, and casts leaf-shaped shadows (the shadow program cuts out the same atlas). The dense forests (14,000 trees) use the light versions (two cones, five cards).
* **Post pass additions** (Medium and High, `LENS`): a **sun glare** (a bright core, a halo, a thin streak and three ghosts along the line through the middle of the picture; it dims when the sun's pixel is not bright, i.e. behind a cloud or a building), a little **colour fringing** towards the corners, and bloom now only takes the really bright parts (threshold .74-1.0 so the bright sky does not haze everything).
* **Buildings**: a better window tile (frame, mullion, transom, sill, concrete grain), **glass that reflects the sky**, air-conditioning boxes on the roofs of the taller buildings. Palms and trees as above.
* **Look** (the OVERCAST preset: a gloomy, overcast DAY; the story is not written, the mood is just there): the sun is a pale patch behind cloud (`ENV` in `src/30_render.js`: weak, high, grey), the light comes from the whole sky, the horizon is thick grey haze (fog density 1/1150 m, thinning with height) and the haze colour is the sky's horizon colour so distance melts into it. The grade (`tone()` in `src/10_gl.js`) is a filmic curve, desaturated to 72 %, cool in the shadows, a touch of contrast. The sky has two cloud layers moving at different speeds (a third octave on the higher tiers), darker where thick, a diffuse bright patch where the sun is. **Roads are wet**: darker asphalt, puddles (a noise field) that act like mirrors, fine ripples, a fresnel reflection of the sky, and a pool of light plus a long view-aligned reflection streak for each of the lights near the car (`uLt`: the car's headlight pool and tail light, the nearest street lamps, the nearest traffic lights; 4 / 6 / 8 on Low / Medium / High). Street lamps are lit (emissive heads, a glow sprite and a pool of light on the road), about a quarter of the windows are lit (a hash per window, warm or cool), downtown and the Valley have 125 neon signs (emissive boxes + glow sprites that flicker), cars have glowing head and tail lights (positions read from the baked mesh's emissive material) and headlight beams on the road, police cars have flashing glow on the siren. Smoke is soft noisy puffs, there are sparks from every crash (`IMP`), and tyre spray off the wet road at speed.
* **Car shader**: the baked garage mesh (it already is the high-detail bake: 9,474 triangles for the body, 1,768 and 1,876 per wheel pair) gets a fake environment (dark wet ground, bright hazy horizon, overcast dome, a soft patch where the sun is, a long overhead softbox for the clear-coat highlight line), a second clear-coat reflection, metallic flakes, road dirt up the lower body, brake lights that glow harder on the brakes. Damage dents are still real vertex displacement (see Damage).
* **Graphics tiers and post pass** (`src/31_gfx.js`, `src/32_shadow.js`, `src/41_fx.js`): the scene is drawn into an off-screen target at the render scale and one pass puts it on the canvas: **FXAA** always, **speed blur** (radial, from about 45 m/s, more with nitro, the car stays sharp) and **bloom** (bright parts at quarter size, two blur passes, added back) on the higher tiers, plus a vignette. **Low**: render scale 0.55-0.85, no shadow map (the blob shadow), 4 wet-road lights, up to 26 lamp glows. **Medium**: scale 0.6-0.95, speed blur, a 512 x 512 sun shadow map over 68 m around the car (the car and the traffic cast), 6 lights, 48 glows. **High**: scale 0.7-1.0, bloom, a 1024 x 1024 map over 92 m that also takes the buildings, trees and lamp posts, 8 lights, 80 glows, 1.25 x the traffic. The shadows are soft and weak (the sun is behind cloud): a 2 x 2 or 3 x 3 filter, strength 0.5, the grid is anchored to the world so nothing shimmers. **Auto / High / Low** (the GFX button, or `?q=auto|high|low`, remembered in `localStorage`; `?tier=0|1|2` pins a tier for testing): in Auto the tier is chosen from MEASURED frame cost, never from the phone model. A frame rate capped at the screen rate says nothing about how much room is left, so every 2.6 s a probe times 8 frames with a forced wait for the GPU (a 1-pixel readback) and the median is the real cost of a frame (CPU + GPU, not overlapped, so a bit pessimistic). Rules that keep a phone from running flat out: step DOWN at 13.5 ms or when frames arrive late (resolution first, then the tier; a tier that failed is locked out for 70 s); step UP only if the estimated cost one step up stays under 9.5 ms (57 % of a 60 fps frame) twice in a row; between 9.5 and 13.5 nothing changes. Auto starts on Medium. The numbers (9.5, 13.5, the tier cost ratios 1 : 1.35 : 1.9) are design values, **not measured on a phone**.
* **Rendering**: terrain is a GPU clip-map (6 rings, one draw each) displaced from a 16-bit height texture; roads are ribbons with analytic
  lane markings; buildings/trees/barriers are merged per 128 m cell and culled by distance + frustum (the haze lets the draw distances be shorter: roads 1.9 km, buildings 1.25 km on Medium); sky, fog and water are shaders.
  About 50-140 draw calls and 75-115k triangles per frame depending on tier and place. No reflections of buildings in the road (the sky and the lights are reflected).
* **Cars** (`src/70_carmesh.js` the generator, `src/71_cars.js` install, `src/72_roster.js` the 30 cars, `src/51_garage.js` the menu): there are **30 made-up supercars**, none a real car and none with a real name, each in the spirit of a real one (a rear-engine coupe like a 911, mid-engine V8 and V10 wedges, front-engine V12 grand tourers, American muscle, lightweight British carbon cars, Japanese all-wheel-drive and hybrid cars, hypercars). Every body is made at start-up from a few numbers by one generator (about 25 ms each): a loft of smooth cross-sections (underbody, flanks, shoulder, bonnet and deck, with a smooth normal everywhere, so no flat polygon facets), wheel wells that slope into the body, a glasshouse (windscreen, side windows with slanted pillar edges, roof, rear glass or engine cover), headlights (round, oval, slit, LED, pop-up), tail lights (bar, wide bar, round, quad, slit), intakes, vents, door lines, mirrors, bonnet duct, engine-cover louvres, diffuser, exhaust tips, wings (lip, ducktail, GT wing, big wing) and stripes, plus real wheels (tyre profile, rim barrel, 5-14 spokes or a mesh or a disc, brake disc and caliper) that spin and steer. about 7-9k triangles per car (and the 4 wheels), 17 materials (paint takes the chosen colour, glass, trim, carbon, chrome, emissive lights, rubber, rim, caliper, exhaust). **Looks pass** (batch D): sharper shoulders and fuller haunches (`flare` .05), a blunter tail with a flat face for the lights and plate, black pillars with a painted roof (a "floating roof"), a **headliner** (an opaque dark ceiling under the roof and rear glass, so the cabin is not an open dish seen from above), a rear window that stops part-way down the slope, darker tinted glass (alpha .66), raised and wider panel lines (they used to z-fight into dotted lines), light bars smoothed (they used to zigzag), a diffuser that stays inside the tail, thicker wing stays, richer paint (saturation 1.3, a crisp horizon line in the clear coat, less horizon haze washing the flanks) and smaller headlight glare. **Detail** (`cgDetails`, `cgDetails2`, `cgInterior`): door cut lines and handles, bonnet and engine-lid shut lines, window trim, fuel flap, badges, number plates, grilles (wide mesh, twin, horseshoe, slit), daytime running lights, fender gills, bonnet scoops, front canards, roof fins, flying buttresses, and **an interior you can see through the tinted glass**: the glass is drawn as its own blended pass after the sky (alpha 0.5 and more at grazing angles) so the dashboard, the steering wheel, the seats (a centre driving seat in one car) and the rear shelf show through; wheels have a sidewall with lettering ribs, a crown with grooves, a deep rim barrel, five different spoke designs (spoke, split, mesh, turbine, disc), a hub, lug nuts, a drilled brake disc and a caliper. Each car has its own set of these (`CAR_EXTRA` in `72_roster.js`) and its own interior colour. The first car, **Kestrel S**, is the rear-engine coupe you start with. **Cars are drawn with the same dent engine as before** (see Damage). The `CARS` button (or `C`) opens the **car menu**: the world stands still and the camera circles the car, with the car's figures as bars (top speed, acceleration, grip, brakes, lightness), 8 paint colours, arrows and a map of dots to browse all 30. **How cars are earned is not decided** (like the money system), so **for now all 30 cars are unlocked** (`CAR_ALL_DEFAULT` in `src/51_garage.js`; the menu's `UNLOCK ALL CARS` switch turns it off to see the locked state). The earning system will set it to false and call `carUnlock(id)` (the hook) as cars are earned; only the first car is owned without it, `ECON.carPrice(id)` is null and a locked car says "How to earn this car is not decided yet". `?car=N` starts in car N. What you own, drive and the paint are saved (`localStorage`).
* **Handling per car** (`carHandling` in `src/72_roster.js`): each car carries the figures of the kind of car it is inspired by (power, mass, which wheels drive, top speed, 0-100 km/h, best lateral g, braking distance from 100 km/h) and they are turned into the numbers of the driving model (`HND` in `40_car.js`) relative to the first car, which keeps exactly the old numbers: top speed (compressed, 75 % of the difference, so a 440 km/h car is not twice as fast as a 308 km/h one), acceleration, cornering grip (and a little more aero grip for the cars with downforce), brakes, agility (lighter and shorter turns in faster), and the drive layout: all-wheel drive grips a little more and slides less, rear-wheel-drive front- and mid-engine cars break traction under throttle at low speed the more power they have for their weight (`HND.powerOS`), mid-engine cars turn in sharper and let go sooner. `node racer/tests/rcars.js` measures all 30 on flat ground and compares the order with the real figures (rank correlation 0.97-1.0 for top speed, 0-100, braking and grip). It is an arcade model: the figures decide the character, they are not a simulation of those cars.
* **Driving** (`HND` constants at the top of `src/40_car.js`): grip first, like the Need for Speed arcade games. Steering is limited by a tyre budget
  (about 1.4-2.3 g), so at speed full lock runs wide instead of sliding. Speed is shown in mph. Tuned like a sports car: firm brakes (60 mph to a stop in about 1.2 s) and quick turn-in.
  **Drift pedal**: a *tap* kicks the tail out (a slide of about 15-25 degrees towards the way you steer, or the side you last steered when the stick is centred) that carries on for about
  half a second after the finger lifts, then a stability assist straightens the car; the stick picks a bigger slide angle. The *longer you hold* it the harder it brakes (a light drag at first, the full 24 m/s^2
  locked-wheel brake after 1.2 s), and the slide angle the stick can ask for grows as the car slows, so a full-stick hold turns the car right round (about 170 degrees in 1.2 s at 60 mph, 2 s at 100, 2.9 s at 140).
  **Any slide is a drift**: points, nitro, tyre smoke and skid marks come from the slide angle (more than about 8 degrees at over 22 mph), not from the pedal, so a slide that carries on after a tap, after a landing or on grass counts too;
  a half-second grace keeps the combo alive across a change of direction. The DRIFT and AIR / JUMP readouts are small, count up live, stay about two seconds after the move ends, then float up and fade out.
  **Energy**: everything is per unit mass, with speed measured along the ground. Gravity pulls the car along the slope exactly (`a = -g m/(1+m^2)`, also sideways down a camber), so climbing turns speed into height
  and descending turns height into speed (coasting over a 30 m hill loses exactly `g h` plus drag; the test sums kinetic + potential + drag work to within 4 %). Brakes, the drift and bumps turn kinetic energy into heat.
  **Air**: the car follows the ground only while the ground pushes up on it. Over a crest of vertical curvature k the ground needs a downward acceleration of `k v^2`; once that is more than gravity the tyres lose the road and
  the car flies on ballistically with the velocity it had (energy conserved, only a little air drag; the engine revs freely and the nose follows the arc). It lands as soon as it meets the ground again: the part of the velocity that points into the ground is
  absorbed (that energy, half the normal speed squared, is lost: shake, camera bob and a small extra scrub above 12 m/s), the rest is kept, so a flat landing keeps all its horizontal speed, a landing on a down slope turns the fall into
  forward speed and one on an up slope loses some. Crossing a crest of height h and length L launches a car above about `sqrt(g L^2 / (2 pi^2 h))`; there are three groups of *jump hills* (6 m, 68 m crests, about 68 mph to take off,
  a 1 s / 50 m flight at 100 mph) on the ring's south straight, the southern highway (two groups) and the east link, marked JUMPS on the map. Also off any bank or bridge edge, and over real hills on the mountain roads.
  Cars stay on the road over small bumps, the body leans and dives. **Nitro zoom**: on a tap the lens widens by 0.26 rad and the camera drops back 2 m, both as springs (13 and 9 rad/s, a little overshoot), so the take-off is quick and punchy but never a jump; when it ends they snap back the same way; the speed blur follows. **The camera follows the car as it is DRAWN** (interpolated between the 120 Hz physics steps; before, the car jittered by up to 0.7 m against the camera at speed), the boost eases in and out (about 0.15 s / 0.3 s: push, top speed, camera pull-back and field of view all follow one number, `C.boostK`), the camera's turning is a critically damped spring and the drift look-ahead eases in over a third of a second (`rcam.js` measures it). Nitro is filled by drifting and is a latch: one tap starts it, it runs until the tank is empty (tap again or brake to stop). Building, barrier and bank collisions, respawn.
* **Controls**: touch (drag the left half to steer; GAS and BRAKE pedals bottom right, DRIFT above brake, NITRO above gas; tap the minimap (top left) for the world map;
