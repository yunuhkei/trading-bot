# Racer prototype

A free-roam arcade racer in **one self-contained HTML file** (`Racer_Prototype.html`, no libraries, no network).
It is built from the modules in `racer/src` by `python3 racer/build.py` (the output is committed so you can just open it).

* **Map**: one fixed county (a 10.2 km square heightmap with 5 m cells, 8.5 km of it land: the sea is to the west and south, cliffs and a beach to the east and north), roads authored in plan view only (`src/20_map.js`), heights follow the land and the land is carved around the roads:
  a ~10 km highway ring with sweeping bends (and a long straight), a mountain pass with switchbacks, a twisty coast road (Highway 1 style) with an overpass,
  a long straight on the southern plains, an east link, and the county's districts, inspired by San Francisco and California:
  **Downtown** = two street grids (one axis-aligned, one rotated to follow the diagonal **Market Street**, like SF), a tower district with a pyramid tower,
  Victorian row houses on **Nob Hill / Russian Hill** (grades over 20 percent) with a hairpin **Lombard Street** block, **Twin Peaks** with a winding road,
  **Golden Gate Park** (a long green strip with a lake and a park drive); **the Valley** = LA style wide palm-lined boulevards with strip malls, parking lots and stucco houses;
  **Mulholland** = a very twisty ridge road in the hills; **Santa Cruz** = a beach town on the south coast (bungalows, motels, a boardwalk with a Ferris wheel,
  lifeguard towers, umbrellas and a pier you can drive out on); **Palm Springs** = a desert resort town of wide palm boulevards (mid-century hotels round pools,
  a golf course, villas with pools).
  **The wider county** (`src/20_world.js`, terrain in `baseH`): a northern range with snow above about 360 m, a 75 m mesa in the north-east cut by a winding canyon (the canyon road follows the floor to a turnaround on the rim),
  a desert lake, a farm lake, and five small towns plus two industrial areas that fill the gaps (**Pacifica** on the coast road, **Fresno** farm town, **Barstow** desert town, **Big Pines** mountain village on a terrace in the foothills,
  the **harbour** industrial area, **Mojave Works**), each a street grid ringed by a boulevard like the districts (`makeTown`), with farms (barn, silo, house, shed, field) along the farm roads, more suburbs round the Valley and the towns,
  and 17 **safe houses** (a garage with a tall green sign, green dots on the map; behaviour comes later). New highways: the east highway to Barstow and the loop back through the refinery and the southern highway, the north highway over the mountain-pass loop to Big Pines, the farm roads.
  **Network** (one connected road system, checked by a script that reports roads lying on top of each other, dead ends and disconnected pieces): every district is ringed
  by a boulevard (`perimeter()`), every street of the district ends on it, and the highways join the boulevards: Geary and the park drive meet downtown's boulevard,
  the boulevard road runs from downtown across the hills to the ring, the Valley joins it and the ring, the coast road runs from Market Street's south-west end
  round the coast to the southern highway (past the Santa Cruz link), the east link joins the ring to the southern highway, Palm Springs joins the east link and the southern
  highway, Mulholland runs from the ring to the boulevard road and the mountain pass leaves the ring and returns to it. Branches leave a road along its side and peel away
  (`rampPts`) or end as a T on its edge (`edgePt`), so no road is built on top of another; where roads really cross (coast road over the ring) there is an overpass
  (found and built automatically, `fixCrossings`).
  Collision: **metal guard rails** (world 1e, `RAILS` and `RAIL_IN` in `src/21_terrain.js`, the response in `carStep`): along the divided highways (open for 60-70 m round every junction and in front of every garage), wherever the ground falls more than 5.5 m beside a road, and on bridges; a galvanised W-beam (its top 0.78 m above the road) on posts every 4 m, its face 1.7 m beyond the asphalt so the shoulder stays drivable. Hitting one is metal on metal, not clay: the car's corner nearest the rail is kept off its face (the car's angle counts, so a nose-first hit stops at the nose), the hit bounces a little (restitution .12) and scrapes (friction .06 of the hit), a glancing hit turns the car along the rail so it slides on (35 / 60 m/s at 6-12 degrees: it keeps 97-100 % of its speed; 25 degrees 87-99 %, 45 degrees 55-64 %, 75 degrees stops it), rubbing along it costs 2 m/s2, sparks stream from the contact and there is a harsh scrape sound; the damage is the energy of the hit (the speed into the rail, in full) plus a fifth of the rubbing (src 4 in `IMP`, so a gentle scrape counts too): 53-63 kJ at 6 degrees, 220-540 kJ at 25, 0.9-2.6 MJ at 75 (`rphys` I). The rail response is the same with `?hnd=classic`. buildings (also the rotated ones), poles, trees and bridge piers are solid
  (with height ranges, so the deck of a bridge is not blocked by what is under it); steep banks are walls.
  Hills: the road-surface lookup picks the nearest road segment at the car's own level (never by height alone, which made the ground a staircase), every city street takes
  its height from one shared smoothed height field (so crossing streets are flush), connector roads blend to the street they join, the body height follows the ground
  closely and the ground cannot launch the car faster than the slope allows. The tests that measure this are in `racer/tests/` (see its README for how to run them and the baseline numbers the driving feel must keep). Everything is deterministic.
* **Junctions** (world logic 1a; `tJoin`, `findJunctions`, `JUNC`, `PLACES` in `src/20_map.js`, test `tests/rnet.js`). The audit of the old network found: a 1 m strip of shoulder across the mouth of all 30 side roads (their ends stopped 1 m short of the road they joined); the coast road running onto the southern highway at 12 degrees and the mountain pass starting as a blunt second road beside the ring; height steps where roads met (0.56 m at the lookout loop, 0.53 m on the Pacifica link, 0.32 m in Golden Gate Park, and 0.3-0.8 m at the mouth corners of mountain roads because the parent road climbs while the branch is flat across); two pairs of highways meeting the ring 60 and 130 m apart instead of at one crossroads; 25 lane directions that could only turn right at their end (left turns across a highway were forbidden everywhere); the Valley with no road south (the harbour 1.5 km away was a 5 km drive). Now: **every branch runs square onto its parent and ends on the parent's centre line** (like the city streets always did), so asphalt always overlaps asphalt; the last metres take the parent's height exactly (projected, not the nearest sample) and a **cross slope** (`r.cf`, also used by the mesh, the physics, the carving and the lane heights) so the mouth lies on the parent even where it climbs; **junction boxes**: around every road end that sits on another road the lane lines and the median fade out on both roads (`JUNC` drives it), and left turns are allowed there (the median is open); the boulevard road and the east highway, the Valley's east road and the east link, and the two north highways now meet at **crossroads**; a new road (`las`) runs from the Valley south to the ring. `PLACES` lists the 10 cities and towns with their boulevards. No ramps: every join is a T or a crossroads (they get traffic lights in 1d). The coast road still crosses the ring on its overpass with no connection there. The only dead end is the pier head (`DEAD_OK`).
* **Curbs and sidewalks** (world 1b; `CURB_H` 15 cm, `SWW` 3.6 m in `src/00_util.js`, `buildSidewalks` / `swOn` in `src/22_build.js`). Every city street and boulevard (`r.sw`) has a raised sidewalk behind a real curb instead of a gravel shoulder: the road ribbon has 8 points across (skirt, sidewalk, curb top, asphalt edge, mirrored), the shader draws the curb face in shadow, a pale curb stone and paving slabs. Where another road's asphalt crosses the sidewalk it is cut, at the exact station (found by bisection, kept in `r.swB`), with a vertical end face, so corners are square and mouths are open. Lamp posts and street trees stand on the sidewalk by the curb. Inside a city the ground beyond the curb is at sidewalk level (it used to lie half a metre below the street). **Driving**: the sidewalk is 15 cm up in `groundAt` (`RQ.cb` = on a sidewalk); a wheel that climbs jolts the car (shake, a dip) and costs 0.7 % of the speed on top of what the step takes (all four wheels at 20 degrees: about 4 % at 9 m/s, 8-11 % at 15-30 m/s); dropping off is a smaller bump (`rphys` J).
  **City crossings are landings** (`cityLand` in `src/20_map.js`): streets were flat across, so where two crossed on a hill the edge of one stood up to 1.6 m above the other (a slab across a 21 % San Francisco street). Now each crossing is one nearly level plane (each street's own grade limited to 8 %), the streets ease back to their profile over 14 m and take the plane's cross slope: steep streets climb in steps like the real city. **One street family per grid edge** (`gridStreets`): where a grid is not square to the edge that cuts it, only the family that meets the edge more squarely opens onto it; the other stops as a T on the last street it crosses (this removed the junction clusters on the downtown boulevard and Market Street: three junctions within 25 m, the old 'known weak spot' of the traffic). A road end with only one way on gives that turn to every lane.
  **Road LOD**: a road piece that is wholly further than `R.roadLod` (90 m Low, 130 m Medium / High) is drawn from a lighter index list (every second sample, three strips across), and buildings are merged per 256 m cell instead of 128 m. With curbs, sidewalks and the three new cities the frame is still lighter than before: see `rbudget`.
* **Three more cities** (world 1c; `CITY_VEG`, `CITY_SAC`, `CITY_SJ`, `makeTown` kinds `vegas` / `cap` / `tech`): **Las Vegas** in the south-east desert (the Strip, a 12 m boulevard, down its middle; hotel slabs in gold, black glass and pink on podiums with lit crowns and sign pylons, a black pyramid with a beam of light, a needle tower, low casinos with marquees, a chapel, palms), reached by a desert highway from Barstow (`vg1`) and a road from Mojave Works (`vg2`); **Sacramento** north of the ring (red and brown brick blocks with stone cornices and awnings, a white capitol with a gold dome and columns in a park, a square with a bandstand, brick row houses, oaks), joined to the north highway (`sc1`), the ring (`sc2`) and the mountain pass (`sc3`); **San Jose** inside the ring (a ring-shaped glass headquarters in a park, low glass campuses with white bands on lawns, car parks under solar roofs, white data halls, apartment blocks), joined to the boulevard road (`sj1`) and the ring (`sj2`). Each has a garage (20 safe houses now), its own ground colour, traffic density and name on the HUD, and is in `PLACES` (so the network test routes to it).
* **Traffic lights** (world 1d; `src/24_signals.js`): 429 intersections in every city and town have lights (1,503 approaches, 3,004 heads). An approach is one road direction arriving at a junction (a lane-graph junction group); approaches within 24 m form an intersection; with three or more approaches and two axes it gets a two-phase controller (phase 0 along the biggest road, phase 1 across it): green 14 / 9 s (11 / 11 when the roads are equal), yellow 3 s (3.5 on main roads), all red 1.2 s, each intersection at its own offset; time is `SIG.t`, advanced in `worldStep`, so it is deterministic. Where the main road is a highway there is no light (the side road gives way, as before). **Cars**: stop at the line on red (3 m before the crossing road's asphalt) unless it is too late to stop, stop on yellow only if they can do it comfortably (under 3.2 m/s2), queue behind each other, and go on green; cars waiting at a red are a parked obstacle for the right-of-way test (so a green car never gives way to them), are not taken for a jam and do not try to pass the queue; no car is spawned where it could not stop for a red ahead; chasing cops ignore the lights. **Looks**: a pole on the right-hand corner at the stop line with a head at 2.8 m and an arm over the road with a second head at 5.6 m (dark housing, three lenses); the lit lamp is a glow sprite with plain blending so red, yellow and green read in sun (the 64 nearest heads that face the camera, no extra draw calls). The player is not affected and running a red is not a crime.
* **Roads are smooth**: the height profile of every road (not the city streets, which follow a smoothed height field) is smoothed with a 28 m binomial filter after the grade limit (`vSmooth` in `src/20_map.js`, ends held exactly so roads still meet flush): the slope clamps used to leave kinks (a slope flipping between +22 % and +6 %, crests only 16 m in radius that threw the car into the air on the mountain pass). No crest tighter than 35 m remains except the designed jump hills (39 m).
* **Lanes**: lane width is one constant (`LANE_W`, 3.6 m in `src/00_util.js`); `laneInfo(hw,style)` gives lanes per direction from the road width, and both the road shader (painted dashed lines, centre line / median) and the traffic lane graph use it, so the paint and the AI lanes always agree. Markings fade towards junctions.
* **Lane graph** (`src/23_lanes.js`, built once at boot in about 0.1 s, 482 lane edges, 2,377 turn links, 434 lane-km, 2 MB): one centre-line per lane per direction for every road except the patches (Lombard Street, the pier), stored in travel order. Junctions are found where a road end touches another road (T, ramp, end to end, three roads at one point) and where two roads cross at the same level; a turn is a short cubic curve from one lane to another (right turns from the outer lane, left turns from the inner lane, a lane that runs out of road may turn from any lane; no left turns across a highway median, so highways get a U-turn crossover about every 500 m; dead ends get a U-turn; a curve that would be shorter than 8 m is pushed further out). Each lane point has a comfortable speed from the road type, curvature and grade. `node racer/tests/lanecheck.js` checks it (the road directions form one strongly connected piece, so a car can never get trapped).
* **Traffic** (`src/60_traffic.js` simulation, `src/61_trafficgl.js` cars): up to 56 cars (`?tmax=N` changes it, `?traffic=0` turns it off) spawned out of sight 140-430 m from the player and removed beyond 540 m (or behind the player past 300 m); more cars in town (downtown 56, the Valley 51, towns 37, highways about 22-27, mountain roads about 19-22). Each car has a horizon (12 points, 6-10 m apart, 66-110 m ahead) along its lane and the turn it picked at the next junction (a seeded hash, so it never changes its mind); IDM car following with a speed profile from the lane speeds ahead; it gives way by comparing its rectangle with every other car's rectangle at the same moment over the next 4 s (road class and turn type decide who goes, then who is nearer; a car that has given way keeps doing so for a second; anything about to touch while still moving brakes whatever the priority); it changes lane to get into the lane of its turn, and to get past a slow car; swerves and brakes for the player; steers by pure pursuit (a kinematic bicycle). A car that has been waiting for a few seconds goes anyway, and a jam that lasts 12-22 s away from the player is dissolved. Five body types (sedan, hatch, SUV, van, police car) in 12 colours with head and tail lights (brake lights light up), drawn instanced with two levels of detail: cars within 46 m of the camera (30 m on Low) use a **detailed body made by the same generator as the player's cars** (about 6.5k triangles: rounded flanks with a shoulder crease, glass, head and tail lamp clusters, indicators, grille, plate, mirrors, window trim, door lines, wheels with a dark dish and spokes; SUVs have roof rails and dark cladding, vans dark cladding), the rest the old 200-triangle body; one draw per type and level (up to 10 draws). Everything is deterministic (one seeded generator, fixed 60 Hz tick shared with the 120 Hz player step).
* **Crashes**: a hit is a real collision between two rectangles (mass 1.1-2.3 t, restitution .26, friction .32) with an impulse at the contact point, so a hit car spins. A light bump just slows the car; above 2.5 kJ it becomes a wreck (a free body: slides, spins, stops at walls and trees, stays up to 30 s and goes away sooner once out of sight). Every impact is recorded in `IMP` (`40_car.js`): source (0 building/tree/pole, 1 steep bank, 2 landing, 3 traffic), closing speed, **energy lost in joules**, contact point and normal, the car's zone (0 front, 1 rear, 2 left, 3 right), the other mass. The damage system reads `IMP`.
* **Damage** (`src/62_damage.js`): every impact in `IMP` adds its energy to one of five zones (front, rear, left, right, roof; a zone is destroyed by `DZ_CAP` = 650 / 520 / 420 / 420 / 320 kJ, a sedan at 24 m/s into a parked car is about 180 kJ; 14 % of a hit spills into the neighbouring zones; scrapes below 3.5 m/s or 8 kJ and landings below 11 m/s do nothing). The zones decide where the dents, the hanging parts and the engine smoke go; **the HEALTH bar (above the NITRO bar) is one pool of crash energy** (`DMG_POOL` = 2.2 MJ: ten 25 m/s hits into a parked car, four into a wall at 30 m/s, two 60 m/s hits into a car; one 40 m/s hit into a parked sedan costs 23 %, a 25 m/s hit 9 %). **Damage never changes how the car drives** (`DMG.spd/acc` stay 1, `pull/leak` 0): it is health and looks only; the car is wrecked only when the health bar is empty (3 % left), never because one zone is crushed (it used to be: a single 40 m/s hit into a van wrecked a car that showed 60 % health). Engine smoke starts at about 30 % damage, the puffs are small and soft, **dark puffs fade out within 4-14 m of the camera** so the chase camera never sits inside a cloud, and fire starts under 30 % health. **Full dents**: the real vertices of the car body near the contact are pushed in along the direction of the hit (Gaussian of the pristine position, up to 0.34 m deep, never more than 0.5 m from where they were, noise a function of position so no cracks open), normals are shaken so the dent catches the light and the paint scuffs; the body buffer is re-uploaded with `bufferSubData` (at once for a big hit, at most every 0.2 s for a run of small ones). Repair puts the pristine copy back. **Traffic cars are dented too**: each car keeps two dents (centre on its body, depth, radius) from its crashes (`trDent` in `60_traffic.js`); the traffic vertex shader pushes the metal in towards the middle of the car, darkens and dulls it there.
* **Safe houses** (`src/63_safe.js`): the garages on the map each have a glowing pad in front of the roller door (green = yours, red = locked). Park on it (under 9 m/s, 1.5 s, the pad slows you) and the car is repaired, the heat is cleared and the spot is saved. The Home Garage near the start is free and owned from the first second; the others are locked. **The money system is NOT decided**: `ECON` is a set of hooks that do nothing (`price(id)` returns null, `canBuy/buy` return false, `repairCost` is 0, `onBusted/onWrecked/onRepair/onUnlock` are empty) and `SAFE.price[id]` is null for every house but Home. Call `safeUnlock(id)` once a house has been paid for; progress is kept in `localStorage` (`racer.save`). Wrecked or busted: a 3.4 s end screen, then you respawn on the pad of the nearest garage you own, repaired and with no cops. `?safe=all` unlocks every garage for testing.
* **Police** (batch 3; `src/64_cops.js` rules and AI, `src/67_police.js` helicopters and spike strips, vehicles in `61_trafficgl.js` / `73_ccars.js`): heat 0-5 (stars). **The heat starts at 0 and only crimes raise it** (`copTrigger(level, why)`, every crime is logged in `COP.crimes`). The old build made you wanted at the start because ANY contact counted: a car that ran into you, or a patrol that nudged you while you pulled out, was booked as your crash. Now a crash is a crime only when **you are at fault** (you were moving into the other car faster than it was moving into you, by more than 2.5 m/s): a light crash you cause is 1 star, a heavy one or a wrecked car 2, driving into a police car 2 (or one more while wanted), wrecking one 4, blowing past a patrol at 120 mph or more (within 55 m) 1. Being hit, being seen, speeding alone: nothing. **Patrols are rare**: one at a time (two on Hard), a new one at most every 50 s (there were 3 / 4 / 5). `rheat.js`: 3 minutes of normal driving from the real start, heat 0, no cop engages.
  **What each star adds** (`STARS` in `64_cops.js`; chasers are for Forgiving / Medium / Hard):

  | stars | chasers | vehicles | tactics |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 2 / 3 / 3 | patrol cars | they follow you with lights and siren, slot in behind and beside you, no deliberate contact |
  | 2 | 4 / 5 / 5 | + interceptors | **ramming**: any chaser within 85 m goes for your back bumper, 10-15 m/s faster than you (4.2 / 3 / 2.2 s between rams) |
  | 3 | 5 / 6 / 7 | + police SUVs | **PIT**: they aim at your rear corner from the side; **roadblocks** of 4 cars across the road every 24 s (Forgiving leaves one out as a gap; fewer on a narrow road) |
  | 4 | 6 / 7 / 8 | interceptors, SUVs, patrol | a **helicopter** with a spotlight (while it is within 220 m you cannot lose the police; 75 s of fuel, the next one 25 s later), **spike strips** laid 150-230 m ahead every 16-20 s, roadblocks of 5, rams 30 % more often |
  | 5 | 8 / 9 / 10 | interceptors, SUVs, **SWAT trucks** | up to 3 helicopters, SWAT trucks (9 t) chase and stand in the middle of roadblocks of 6, **near-constant PIT** (the time between rams x 0.4) |

  **Vehicles**: patrol car (Close Call's cop body), interceptor (low coupe), police SUV, SWAT truck (dark armoured box, 9 t): all with the black and white livery (the SWAT truck all dark with a white band), a light bar with red, clear and blue segments, a push bar, spotlights, an aerial, a blue and gold door stripe. **AI**: a chaser takes the real route to you (A* over the lane graph, recomputed every 1.8 s), goes into free pursuit within 115 m, **leads its target** (aims up to 1.2 s ahead of you, so it cuts corners instead of trailing round them), takes a slot by role (behind, left flank, right flank, ahead to box you in), is pushed out of buildings and props every step (`trStatic`; never inside one in `rpolice`), and when it is **stuck** nose-on to something for a second it **reverses out** turning away and goes again. It matches your speed x 1.1 (up to 105 m/s) with a rubber-band boost, is replaced by a fresh one when it falls 520 m behind. You lose them by being more than 230 m from every chaser and out of sight for 14 s (Forgiving), by reaching a garage, or by wrecking them; boxed in under 6 m/s for 5 s = BUSTED. Presets in `COPD` (`?cops=off|medium|hard`, Forgiving is the default).
  **Spike strips** (`SPIKE`, `spikeCheck`): a dark mat across the whole road with steel teeth and a red flasher at each end, `SPIKE STRIP AHEAD` on the HUD when one is laid. Driving over it **bursts the tyres**: a bang, sparks from the rims, `TYRES BURST`, and **about 4 s of half grip** (cornering and traction x 0.5, back to normal over the last second; a turn at 30 m/s is 48 % of normal, `C.flatT`). **This is the one deliberate exception to "damage never changes how the car drives".** **Helicopters** (`HELI`): navy and white, cabin, tail boom, skids, rotor discs that blur, a spotlight beam and a pool of light on you, the chop of the rotor getting louder as it nears; they fly 32-45 m up; one draw call each (21 boxes, about 250 triangles). **Minimap**: only the police are shown (a patrol a blue dot, a chaser flashing red and blue, a helicopter a ring); all other traffic is off it. Cost of the batch: three more vehicle types in the traffic batches (no extra draw calls), up to 3 helicopter draws at five stars, a few dozen fx quads; `rbudget` is unchanged.
* **Navigation and the map** (`src/65_nav.js`): the **world map is touchable**: drag to move it, pinch (or mouse wheel) to zoom, tap a road to drop a pin, `SET WAYPOINT` to go there (`CLEAR WAYPOINT`, `MY CAR`, `CLOSE MAP`). The route is found with A* over the lane graph (it follows the way traffic really drives: one-way lanes, turns that exist, median crossovers; 3-11 ms for any route on the map), starts in the direction you are heading, and is **drawn on the road** as a pulsing blue ribbon for the next 420 m with a pillar of light at the waypoint; the HUD shows `WAYPOINT 4.4 km` under the minimap and an arrow along the top edge when it is not in front of you; the minimap and the world map show the route and the flag. Leave the route (a wrong turn) and a new route is made from where you are; arrive (26 m) and it clears.
* **NPC damage**: each traffic car keeps up to 3 dents from its crashes (position, depth, radius; the vertex shader pushes the metal in and crumples and dulls it) and a damage total (`c.dmg`, 0..1; a sedan is finished by 170 kJ, a van 280 kJ). From 0.45 its head and tail lights are smashed (dark), from 0.45 it smokes (more from 0.65, fire from 0.85); a light knock (under 32 kJ, damage under 0.72) spins it round and it re-attaches to the nearest lane and **drives on, limping** (slower and weaker the more it is damaged), a big hit finishes it as a wreck.
* **Drift taps build up** (`40_car.js`, `carStep`): tap the drift pedal again within 1.6 s of the last tap while still sliding and the slide gets harder each time (`C.tapN` 0..5, smoothed to `C.dbK` 0..1): the slide angle the tap kicks out grows up to 2.5 times, the slide window 2.2 times, the yaw-rate cap 1.55 times, grip in the slide drops by 40 %, and the drift points go up to 3 times (the toast shows `x2`, `x3` ...). The build-up fades 1.6 s after the last tap, taps made slower than that start again and a single tap or a hold feels exactly as before (`dbK` is 0). On flat ground at 94 mph: one tap 10 degrees, three taps 20, five taps 48 (`rdrift3.js`).
* **Damage you can see** (`src/66_parts.js`, `cgParts` in `70_carmesh.js`): looks only, nothing here is read by the driving. The generator finds the **bonnet, boot lid, both doors (with their windows), both bumpers (with the lights) and both mirrors** by where each triangle sits on the car (quads together, so edges follow the grid), copies them into vertices of their own and puts a dark liner a few centimetres inside the skin behind every door, bonnet and boot (so an open door shows a dark cabin). The index buffer is ordered by part, so each part is one run, drawn with its own hinge matrix (the player's car is 1 + up to 8 draws, the glass the same). Each part follows a target taken from the damage zones with thresholds that change after every repair (`partTarget`): the bonnet lifts as the front is hurt (up to 63 degrees) and flutters at speed, the boot lid the same for the rear, a door opens on the side that was hit (up to 80 degrees, held back by the wind at speed, swinging when hit), bumpers swing and sag, mirrors fold; a hit kicks the nearest parts and a latch that broke holds the part a little open. **Parts tear off** (`partDetach`): a bonnet or boot lid after a hit over 300 kJ with the zone past 60 % or at 90 %, a bumper over 55-75 % damage, a door over 85 % (or 300 kJ at 70 %), a mirror from 25 kJ; the piece keeps part of the car's speed, is thrown (bonnet up and back, doors sideways) and tumbles under gravity, bounces and slides on the real ground (`groundAt`), lies there, and goes after about 19 s (at most 10 pieces). **Glass cracks** (`uCr` in the car shader): a cell pattern of lines spreads from a point as the zone beside the window takes damage (windscreen from the front and roof, rear glass from the rear, each side from its side). **Lights die** one side at a time (`uDm`: the emissive head or tail light goes dark, the glow sprite and half the headlight pool go too). Repair (`dmgReset`) puts everything back on and starts a new random set. **Traffic and police cars** open their bonnet (front hit), boot (rear) or door (side) once the car is hurt: part numbers ride in the high bits of the material byte (`part * 16 + material`), flag bits 16/32/64/128 in the instance flags, and the vertex shader swings the part about the hinge (`uHg`, per body type) with a little flutter; the liner is part of the same mesh.

A frame rate capped at the screen rate says nothing about how much room is left, so every 2.6 s a probe times 8 frames with a forced wait for the GPU (a 1-pixel readback) and the median is the real cost of a frame (CPU + GPU, not overlapped, so a bit pessimistic). Rules that keep a phone from running flat out: step DOWN at 13.5 ms or when frames arrive late (resolution first, then the tier; a tier that failed is locked out for 70 s); step UP only if the estimated cost one step up stays under 9.5 ms (57 % of a 60 fps frame) twice in a row; between 9.5 and 13.5 nothing changes. Auto starts on Medium. The numbers (9.5, 13.5, the tier cost ratios 1 : 1.35 : 1.9) are design values, **not measured on a phone**.

* **Handling per car** (`carHandling` in `src/72_roster.js`): each car carries the figures of the kind of car it is inspired by (power, mass, which wheels drive, top speed, 0-100 km/h, best lateral g, braking distance from 100 km/h) and they are turned into the numbers of the driving model (`HND` in `40_car.js`) relative to the first car, which keeps exactly the old numbers: top speed (compressed, 75 % of the difference, so a 440 km/h car is not twice as fast as a 308 km/h one), acceleration, cornering grip (and a little more aero grip for the cars with downforce), brakes, agility (lighter and shorter turns in faster), and the drive layout: all-wheel drive grips a little more and slides less, rear-wheel-drive front- and mid-engine cars break traction under throttle at low speed the more power they have for their weight (`HND.powerOS`), mid-engine cars turn in sharper and let go sooner. `node racer/tests/rcars.js` measures all 30 on flat ground and compares the order with the real figures (rank correlation 0.97-1.0 for top speed, 0-100, braking and grip). * **Driving** (`HND` constants at the top of `src/40_car.js`): grip first, like the Need for Speed arcade games. Steering is limited by a tyre budget
  (about 1.4-2.3 g), so at speed full lock runs wide instead of sliding. Speed is shown in mph. Tuned like a sports car: firm brakes (60 mph to a stop in about 1.2 s) and quick turn-in.
  **Drift pedal**: a *tap* kicks the tail out (a slide of about 15-25 degrees towards the way you steer, or the side you last steered when the stick is centred) that carries on for about
  half a second after the finger lifts, then a stability assist straightens the car; the stick picks a bigger slide angle. The *longer you hold* it the harder it brakes (a light drag at first, the full 24 m/s^2
  locked-wheel brake after 1.2 s), and the slide angle the stick can ask for grows as the car slows, so a full-stick hold turns the car right round (about 170 degrees in 1.2 s at 60 mph, 2 s at 100, 2.9 s at 140).
  **Any slide is a drift**: points, nitro, tyre smoke and skid marks come from the slide angle (more than about 8 degrees at over 22 mph), not from the pedal, so a slide that carries on after a tap, after a landing or on grass counts too;
  a half-second grace keeps the combo alive across a change of direction. The DRIFT and AIR / JUMP readouts are small, count up live, stay about two seconds after the move ends, then float up and fade out.
  **Energy**: everything is per unit mass, with speed measured along the ground. Gravity pulls the car along the slope exactly (`a = -g m/(1+m^2)`, also sideways down a camber), so climbing turns speed into height
  and descending turns height into speed (coasting over a 30 m hill loses exactly `g h` plus drag; the test sums kinetic + potential + drag work to within 4 %). Brakes, the drift and bumps turn kinetic energy into heat.
  **Air**: the car follows the ground only while the ground pushes up on it. Over a crest of vertical curvature k the ground needs a downward acceleration of `k v^2`; once that is more than gravity the tyres lose the road and
  the car flies on ballistically with the velocity it had (energy conserved, only a little air drag; the engine revs freely and the nose follows the arc). It lands as soon as it meets the ground again: the part of the velocity that points into the ground is
  absorbed (that energy, half the normal speed squared, is lost: shake, camera bob and a small extra scrub above 12 m/s), the rest is kept, so a flat landing keeps all its horizontal speed, a landing on a down slope turns the fall into
  forward speed and one on an up slope loses some. Crossing a crest of height h and length L launches a car above about `sqrt(g L^2 / (2 pi^2 h))`; there are three groups of *jump hills* (6 m, 68 m crests, about 68 mph to take off,
  a 1 s / 50 m flight at 100 mph) on the ring's south straight, the southern highway (two groups) and the east link, marked JUMPS on the map. Also off any bank or bridge edge, and over real hills on the mountain roads.
  Cars stay on the road over small bumps, the body leans and dives. **Nitro zoom**: on a tap the lens widens by 0.26 rad and the camera drops back 2 m, both as springs (13 and 9 rad/s, a little overshoot), so the take-off is quick and punchy but never a jump; when it ends they snap back the same way; the speed blur follows. **The camera follows the car as it is DRAWN** (interpolated between the 120 Hz physics steps; before, the car jittered by up to 0.7 m against the camera at speed), the boost eases in and out (about 0.15 s / 0.3 s: push, top speed, camera pull-back and field of view all follow one number, `C.boostK`), the camera's turning is a critically damped spring and the drift look-ahead eases in over a third of a second (`rcam.js` measures it). Nitro is filled by drifting and is a latch: one tap starts it, it runs until the tank is empty (tap again or brake to stop). Building, barrier and bank collisions, respawn.
* **Handling review** (`PHYS` and `deflect()` in `src/40_car.js`, measured by `tests/rphys.js`): the starter's `HND` numbers are no longer frozen. Six things were measurably off and are fixed; **the old behaviour is one switch away** (`?hnd=classic`, or `HANDLING: NEW / CLASSIC` in the car menu, remembered; `CLASSIC HANDLING` shows at the bottom of the screen while it is on):
  1. **Small steering inputs were cancelled.** The road assist (the car follows the road with the thumb off the stick) faded out only at 22 % of the stick, so it fought every small correction: at 40 m/s a 12 % stick gave 26 % of the turn it should and 6 % gave nothing, at 65 m/s 6 % steered slightly the WRONG way, and a small stick held for 3 s stopped turning after 1.5 s. Now the assist only works below 1.5-5 % of the stick (thumb off): the stick is a rate control everywhere.
  2. **Walls were clay.** A hit moved the velocity along the wall but left the nose pointing into it, so the tyres scrubbed the speed off and the car hit again and again (15 degrees at 55 m/s: 20 impacts, 62 % of the speed left after 1.5 s; 40 degrees: 11 %). Now a glancing hit (up to about 35 degrees, fading out by 57) turns the car along the wall, a cut bank or a rail and it slides on (15 degrees: 98 %, 25: 83 %, 40: 60 %); 60 and 90 degrees still stop it, the crash energy recorded is the same.
  3. **It spun about its middle at low speed.** Full lock at walking speed turned on a 2 m radius with the tail swinging out at 3.6 m/s. Now the turning circle is 4.2 m (scaled per car by its agility) and below about 15 m/s the car turns about its rear axle like a real one. Above that nothing changes (the tyre budget decides).
  4. **Reverse had no top speed** (48 m/s after 8 s on the brake and still rising): now 11 m/s.
  5. **The launch boost cut out in one step** at 14 m/s (a 1.9 m/s2 jolt): it now fades between 9 and 19 m/s (0-100 km/h is still 2.33 s).
  6. **The car kept turning in the air** at the yaw rate it left the ground with and landed sideways (15 degrees of slide after a jump taken with a little steering): the yaw rate now dies in about a third of a second (7 degrees).
  Not changed, on purpose: brakes and cornering do not share the tyre (full braking and full cornering together, arcade), the yaw response (63 % in 0.11 s, 90 % in 0.2 s), the grip limit, the drift pedal, the jump and landing energy rules, and the camera. The feel tests (`rhandle`, `rturn`, `rdrift2`) give the same numbers as before within rounding.
