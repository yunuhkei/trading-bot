# Hollow Saints: pitch

**One line:** A framed cop hunts the cops, judges and clergy who run his city, and finds out he may have helped them do it.

**Genre:** Open-world crime action with a story-driven noir campaign
**Platform:** Phone browser first (tap a link, no install); desktop browser also works
**Rating:** Mature 17+ / PEGI 18 (graphic violence, blood, strong language and themes)
**Session length:** 10–20 minute missions, built for playing on a phone

## The hook

Six years ago, Detective Elias Crowe was convicted of executing a witness. Tonight he's broken out of a prison transport by the same people who put him there, so they can kill him before he remembers. Every chapter he takes down one of the Hollow Saints, the ring of power that owns Port Calvary. Every chapter he also finds more proof that he was one of them.

## Who it's for

Adults who loved GTA, Max Payne, Sleeping Dogs or *True Detective*, and who want that on their phone without a 10 GB download. They play in short bursts on a commute or in bed, and they want a story that sticks with them.

## What makes it different

1. **You're investigating your own crime.** Crowe doesn't remember the night of the killing, and the evidence keeps pointing at him. He's an unreliable hero in a noir mystery, not a power fantasy, and the ending turns on what he really did.
2. **The enemy is the system itself.** The Saints are cops, judges, a deputy mayor and a bishop. The police chasing you and the villains you hunt are the same organization, so the wanted level is part of the story, not just a punishment. Each chapter is built around one Saint: their district, their people, how they die.
3. **Violence with weight, on a phone.** Real ragdoll physics, blood that stays where it falls, bodies that stay in the street. The city remembers what you did. That's rare on mobile, where most shooters are clean and consequence-free.

## What's strong right now

- **It runs instantly from a link,** with controls built for thumbs: auto-aim, a RUN toggle, and a separate driving layout with a drifting handbrake.
- **The atmosphere lands:** rainy neon city, a harbor, forests, a lighthouse beam, snowfall in the mountains, thunder and sirens.
- **The ragdolls and blood** already feel heavier than most mobile games.
- **The opening is a strong hook:** the escape, the dead informant, and the ledger with Crowe's own handwriting.
- **The island has variety:** city, coast and mountains, plus room for at least 5 chapters.

## What's weak right now (honest)

- **The people are made of boxes.** That's fine for a prototype, but it's the biggest gap between this and a "real" game.
- **There's one gun and one mission.** No weapon variety, no money, nothing to buy or unlock, so free roam has nothing to do yet.
- **There's no saving.** Closing the page loses your progress.
- **The world is empty between set pieces.** No traffic drives on the roads, and there are no interiors or side activities.
- **The story is told only in text cards.** No cutscenes, no character voices, no music.
- **Enemies are basic:** they walk at you and shoot, with no cover or flanking. Cops appear out of nowhere.
- **It's untested on real phones.** Frame rate and the 3-second load are unknown on actual devices, which is the biggest risk for a phone-first game.

## What version 1 needs to be

A 5-chapter campaign of about 3–4 hours, plus a saving system, 4–5 weapons, money and safehouses, traffic, and a few side activities. Next comes Stage 2: a full design document that turns this into a mission-by-mission plan and decides what's in version 1 and what waits.
